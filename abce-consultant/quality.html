The standard is part of the item

In high-criticality service, a component is only as good as the specification it was made to and the documentation that proves it. These are the standards we work to and source against.

What these standards mean here — and what they do not

Standards language is routinely blurred in supplier marketing. We would rather be exact, because the difference changes what you can rely on.

Standards alignment

The standards below are the frameworks our procurement, inspection and documentation practices are built around. This describes how we work.

Certified product sourcing

Products are sourced to the specification that governs the application, and the manufacturer's material and conformity documentation is collected with the goods. The certification belongs to the product and its manufacturer.

Neither of the above is a certification held by {{ site.name }}. Where you require evidence of a certification — ours or a manufacturer's — ask us and we will tell you precisely what exists and provide it where it does. We will not supply a document we cannot stand behind.

How the standard is actually enforced

A specification only protects you if someone checks against it. Four points in our process where that happens.

  1. At enquiry

    Grade, rating, service conditions and required documentation are established and written into the enquiry, so suppliers bid against the same requirement.

  2. At bid evaluation

    Offers are compared on specification compliance first. A cheaper non-compliant offer is not a cheaper offer.

  3. Before despatch

    Dimensional, marking, quantity and packing checks against the purchase order and datasheet, with the documentation pack assembled and checked for consistency.

  4. On receipt

    Goods are received against the order, non-conformances are raised with the supplier, and records are issued with the consignment.

Checked against the order, not the invoice

Safety is a procurement decision

Most of what determines whether a job is safe has already been decided by the time the material reaches site.

  • Load-rated hardware

    Rigging and lifting equipment is load-rated. We source it with the manufacturer certification applicable to the item and confirm what documentation accompanies it before you order.

  • Classified areas

    Equipment for hazardous areas is specified against your area classification, ingress protection and temperature class — not substituted for something that merely fits.

  • HSE supply

    Safety supplies are part of the consumables stream, so the crews doing the work are equipped from the same controlled supply chain as the plant.
