Company overview

We exist because industrial supply goes wrong in predictable ways, and almost none of them are about availability.

A pump is ordered to the wrong casing material and corrodes within a season. A batch of flanges arrives without the material certificates the client's QA department requires, so it cannot be installed. A consignment clears the supplier's gate on time and then sits at a port because a packing list and an invoice disagree about the weight. In each case the part existed, the money was spent, and the work still stopped.

{{ site.name }} takes the whole chain as one scope: define the requirement properly, source it from suppliers assessed for that product, verify it against the specification before it is accepted, and move it through freight, documentation and customs to the site that needs it. One party is accountable for the outcome, which is the only arrangement under which the outcome is reliably good.

Our work spans five product streams — civil and structural bulk materials, mechanical and piping equipment, rigging and lifting gear, electrical systems, and tools and consumables — supported by ten procurement and logistics services.

Operational philosophy

Four working principles. They are not slogans; each one describes something we do differently from a company that simply forwards enquiries to a trader.

  • The specification comes first

    Before an enquiry goes out we establish grade, rating, service conditions, area classification and the documentation the receiving system needs. Most supply failures are specification failures wearing a logistics costume.

  • Verify, then accept

    Goods are checked against the purchase order and the datasheet before acceptance, with documentation collected alongside them. A non-conformance found at the supplier is an inconvenience; the same one found on site is an outage.

  • One chain, one owner

    Procurement, inspection, freight, documentation and clearance are run together. Splitting them across parties creates the gaps that consignments fall into, and nobody who owns only one link can close them.

  • Plan backwards from the date

    When a date cannot move, we plan the movement from that date backwards — supplier readiness, mode, routing, clearance and the final leg — and drive it, rather than reporting delays after they happen.
